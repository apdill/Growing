# Precision limits, not power laws, open the path

Growing a network to near-criticality with homeostatic neurite growth is already published: van Ooyen & van Pelt (1994/96), Abbott & Rohrkemper (2007), Tetzlaff et al. (2010) and especially Kalle Kossio et al. (2018, *PRL*) did it with circular neurite fields. Kossio derived σ = 1 − f0/f_sat, and Zierenberg, Wilting & Priesemann (2018, *PRX*) derived m = 1 − h/r* explicitly. The project's headline relation is therefore a reproduction, not a discovery. The hovering of σ around its set point, and the oversized avalanches launched from supercritical epochs, are also predicted qualitatively by self-organized quasi-criticality (SOqC) theory (Bonachela & Muñoz 2009; Kinouchi et al. 2019). What the literature does **not** contain, as far as these searches reached, is a quantitative account of the *precision* of growth-driven criticality. Nobody has related σ fluctuations to a discrete contact quantum, mapped growth rate against sensor timescale onto both avalanche shape and dynamic range, or done finite-size scaling of a growing network under h ∝ 1/N. Nobody has linked fractal dendritic morphology to network criticality at all. Two of the project's results, the contact-granularity fluctuation floor and the dissociation in which fast growth yields 3/2 laws while slow growth yields the best dynamic range, are the most promising route to a significant paper. That is because they turn known artefacts (pooled sub- and supercritical mixtures, σ ≠ m) into mechanistic results, and they speak directly to the live debate over whether power laws indicate criticality. Morphology is a weak lever. D = 2.5 is not a realizable box-counting dimension in 2D, and homeostatic growth should absorb geometry, so the morphology null is expected rather than surprising. Before committing, the project must resolve a tension with Das & Levina (2019), who predict a 5/4 tail exponent under exactly the drive scaling the project uses. It must also measure the largest eigenvalue Λ and causal m rather than the column-sum σ.

The pattern is clear. The *mean* behaviour of a homeostatically grown network is solved theory. Its *fluctuations*, *finite-size behaviour* and *functional consequences* are not. The rest of this report develops that asymmetry.

## Circular neurite fields already grew critical networks, and Kossio solved the mean

The closest prior work is a single lineage of "neuritic-field" models. In van Ooyen & van Pelt's original rate model, each neuron has one circular field whose overlap sets coupling, and which grows when activity is below a set point and shrinks above it. Networks pass through a **transient connectivity overshoot** before settling, and can oscillate on the growth timescale ([van Ooyen & Butz-Ostendorf 2019](https://anvooyen.home.xs4all.nl/papers/vanooyen_butz_cdns_2019.pdf)) [V-rev]. Abbott & Rohrkemper added Poisson spiking to this scheme. With N = 100 neurons in a unit square and calcium-driven radius growth (k = 0.002 s⁻¹), they obtained s^−3/2 sizes and T^−2 durations ([Abbott & Rohrkemper 2007, *Prog Brain Res*](https://zpkilpat.github.io/appm4370/papers/abbott07.pdf)) [V]. Their criticality was **not generic**: the size exponent ranged from 0.04 to 1.2 across C_target and hit −1.5 only near C_target ≈ 0.08. They called for separate dendritic and axonal fields as future work, still circles [V].

Tetzlaff et al. split each neuron into dendritic and axonal disks with *opposite* growth rules and switched 20% of neurons to inhibitory mid-development. They reproduced a three-phase developmental sequence: a slow rise, a **supercritical connectivity peak**, then pruning to a near-critical state with a size exponent close to −1.5 ([Tetzlaff et al. 2010, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001013)) [V-rev for model details]. Kalle Kossio et al. made the theory exact. Neurons are a linear Hawkes process, coupling is g × overlap area, and radius shrinks by K/f_sat per spike. The grown steady state has **σ = 1 − f0/f_sat**, and avalanches defined as Hawkes clusters follow a Borel distribution ≈ s^−3/2 with cutoff s_c = (σ − ln σ − 1)⁻¹ ([Kalle Kossio et al. 2018, *PRL*](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1811.02861)) [V]. They required f0 ≪ f_sat and slow growth (1/K ≫ τ). They reported coupling fluctuations below 1%, found that freezing growth after convergence changed nothing, and stated that the size distribution is independent of N [V].

In parallel, Zierenberg, Wilting & Priesemann derived the same mean-field relation for synaptic scaling rather than growth: **r = h/(1 − m), so m = 1 − h/r*** ([Zierenberg et al. 2018, *PRX*](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.01479)) [V]. They also found a breakdown. When h/r* falls below about Δt/τ′, with τ′ = τ_hp/k̄, homeostasis cannot track activity, m develops a saw-tooth, and dynamics turn bursty. In vitro bursting corresponds to less than 0.1% input-driven spikes, and in vivo to about 1% [V].

The field's own review is explicit that "the use of circular neuritic fields in all models is a simple yet powerful way to abstract away from detailed neuronal morphology". It lists open problems: the synaptic-element Model of Structural Plasticity (MSP) "has not yet been used to study avalanche dynamics", the analytical role of inhibition, and heterogeneous set-points ([van Ooyen & Butz-Ostendorf 2019, Springer](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-20965-0_7)) [V]. Searches for 2019–2026 follow-ups found reviews ([Zeraati, Priesemann & Levina 2021, *Front Phys*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2021.619661)) [V], abstract topological rewiring ([Landmann, Baumgarten & Bornholdt 2021, *PRE*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33862737/)) [V-abs], and MSP-style rewiring for memory engrams with no criticality analysis ([Gallinaro, Gašparović & Rotter 2022, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.03.27.011171v2.full)) [V-abs]. **No paper combining realistic or fractal branching morphology, contact connectivity and homeostatic growth toward criticality was found.** That conclusion rests on keyword searches. Google Scholar "cited by" lists for Kossio 2018 and Abbott & Rohrkemper 2007 could not be accessed.

One subtlety matters for the project. Kossio's N-independence holds because Hawkes clusters separate overlapping cascades genealogically. The project uses time-binned avalanches, where the total drive N·h sets an extra N-dependent limit: cascades merge unless N·h·⟨T⟩ ≪ 1. Scaling h ∝ 1/N holds total drive fixed, so the project's large-N protocol is the time-binned analogue of Kossio's f0/f_sat → 0 limit. This mapping is an inference, not stated in Kossio. Kossio's slow-growth condition also matters for the project's fast-growth runs: they violate 1/K ≫ τ by design, so their 3/2-like laws are not Kossio's mechanism.

## Hovering is predicted, but its precision floor is not

### SOqC theory explains the oscillation qualitatively, with formulas only for continuous adaptation

Self-organized quasi-criticality is the theoretical home for the project's σ oscillation. With non-conserving or homeostatic feedback, the control variable "does not self-tune to a critical value" but "keeps hovering around it, with excursions of finite amplitude to both sides". Sweeping through the transition induces "approximate or 'dirty' scaling", not bona fide scale invariance (Bonachela & Muñoz 2009, as reviewed in [Buendía et al. 2020, *Front Phys*](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2006.03020)) [V-rev]. Bonachela et al. argued that the Levina–Herrmann–Geisel (LHG) model is generically sub- or supercritical without fine-tuning ([Bonachela et al. 2010, *J Stat Mech*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.3256)) [V-abs].

Kinouchi et al. gave the most quantitative treatment. The adaptive fixed point is a weakly stable focus whose offset is O(1/τ): Γ* ≈ 1 + 2/τ for adaptive gain, and σ* = 1 + (A−1)/(1 + uτ) for LHG synapses. Its eigenvalue modulus is |λ| = 1 − O(1/τ) (0.990, 0.998 and 0.999 at τ = 100, 500 and 1000), and its oscillation frequency is ω ≃ τ^−1/2. Demographic noise excites **stochastic oscillations** that produce both power-law avalanches and supercritical **"dragon king"** events ([Kinouchi et al. 2019, *Sci Rep*](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.05537)) [V]. That is the project's "avalanches starting at high σ are 10–80× larger" in theoretical form. Girardi-Schappo et al. showed homeostatic synapses and thresholds hover with an amplitude that shrinks with N ([Girardi-Schappo et al. 2020, *Phys Rev Research*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09117)) [V-abs]. Menesse et al. showed that separating the timescales of coupling and threshold adaptation sustains SOqC even under external input ([Menesse et al. 2022, *Chaos Solitons Fractals*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.02718v2)) [V-abs].

Finite-size behaviour of the fluctuation amplitude is **contested**. Bonachela et al. reportedly found LHG oscillation amplitude "basically does not change with N". Kinouchi found Δσ flat up to a τ-dependent size N(τ) and decreasing beyond it, with the oscillations vanishing in the thermodynamic limit under τ ∝ N ([Kinouchi et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.05537)) [V for Kinouchi; Bonachela claim via secondary citation]. The project's data, Var(σ) versus N at fixed contact quantum, can contribute directly to this dispute.

### Granularity and growth rate are the unclaimed parameters

Every existing analytic offset scales with a *rate*: 1/τ, or u/τ per spike. **No source found treats a quantized coupling increment, "one contact changes σ by Δσ_c", as the small parameter controlling the fluctuation floor.** Synapse-number-based structural-plasticity rules exist ([eLife reviewed preprint 88376](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/88376)) [U], but none frames a precision limit. This is an absence from a limited search, not a confirmed one. Kossio's continuous overlap removes granularity by construction ([Kossio et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1811.02861)) [V].

Similarly, no paper links plasticity rate to *both* avalanche shape and dynamic range. The closest statements are Buendía's remark that "the choice of parameters (and not only system size) can determine the 'cleanliness' of the observed scaling" ([Buendía et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2006.03020)) [V], and Kuehn's analysis of optimal timescale ratios in adaptive SOC networks ([Kuehn 2012, arXiv:1110.5433](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5433)) [U]. This gap is where the project's slow/fast growth dissociation sits.

The literature supplies a mechanistic reading of that dissociation. Fast growth produces large σ excursions. Pooling avalanches across sub- and supercritical epochs produces apparent power laws, the same mechanism by which slowly varying inhomogeneous Poisson drive yields "approximate power laws with cutoffs comparable to those … observed in experiments" ([Priesemann & Shriki 2018, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006081)) [V]. Meanwhile, dynamic range is maximized when the network sits *steadily* near the transition ([Larremore, Shew & Restrepo 2011, *PRL*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.058101)) [V-abs], ([Shew et al. 2009, *J Neurosci*](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3864-09.2009)) [V-abs]. If this reading holds, the project has a model in which **the 3/2 law and the functional benefit attributed to criticality anticorrelate**. That would be a sharp, falsifiable contribution to the critique literature.

### The m(N) numbers leave a residual worth explaining

Under exact h ∝ 1/N and m = 1 − h/r*, an 8× increase in N should shrink 1 − m 8× as well. The project's values go from 0.052 to 0.012, a 4.3× change, which implies 1 − m ∝ N^−0.71. This is my computation from the stated numbers, and it assumes h was scaled exactly as 1/N from the N = 25 value. If so, the measured m at N = 200 sits about twice as far from 1 as the formula predicts (about 0.012 versus about 0.0065). Four candidate explanations each predict something different. Coalescence bias in macroscopic m is largest in small, dense networks ([Zierenberg et al. 2020, *PRE*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10402)) [V-abs]. Saturation and refractoriness reduce effective transmission. Hovering asymmetry biases the time-averaged m. Finally, a granularity floor could stop the homeostat from resolving σ finer than Δσ_c. Separating these is exactly the precision-limit analysis the field lacks.

Kossio's cutoff formula also shows why fixed-window exponents move with N. It gives s_c ≈ 710 at σ = 0.948, about 1,600 at 0.965 and about 13,800 at 0.988 (my computation from Kossio's Eq. 5). The small-N fits therefore see a cutoff inside their window. The N = 25 exponent of 1.10, *flatter* than 3/2, cannot come from subcritical truncation, which steepens tails. It points instead to merged cascades and supercritical-epoch mixing. Zierenberg 2018 also noted a finite-size bump at large sizes [V].

A further prediction follows from Zierenberg's stability bound. Under h ∝ 1/N, h/r* falls with N, and unless contacts per neuron k̄ grow too, large-N networks will eventually cross h/r* < Δt·k̄/τ_hp into the **bursting regime**, where m saw-tooths and avalanche distributions become peaked ([Zierenberg et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.01479)) [V]. The project should locate its runs relative to this line. Its "quasi-critical oscillation" may be Zierenberg's low-drive breakdown rather than Kinouchi's noise-driven focus, or a mixture of the two.

### Das & Levina predict 5/4 under the project's own drive scaling

This is the sharpest conflict in the literature. Das & Levina show that when external input arrives during avalanches, the asymptotic size exponent changes. With small input φ = o(1/N), P(k) ~ k^−1.5. With moderate input it becomes **k^−1.25**, with a crossover near size φ^−2 below which 1.5 persists. With large input φ = Θ(N), the distribution is Gaussian. They confirm the shift in branching networks at N = 10⁵ ([Das & Levina 2019, *PRX*](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04196)) [V]. Their abstract defines the moderate regime as input scaling as 1/N, while the body uses φ = Θ(1). The likely reconciliation is that φ is a network-level total, so a *per-neuron* rate ∝ 1/N is moderate [U, researchers' inference]. On that reading, the project's h ∝ 1/N protocol is squarely in the moderate regime and should show a 5/4 tail beyond the crossover.

The project's τ rising from 1.10 to 1.41 does not yet contradict this. A fixed window at N ≤ 200 can straddle the crossover, and 1.41 lies between 1.25 and 1.5. The trend can also reflect shrinking hovering amplitude (finer granularity at larger N) rather than an approach to a 3/2 asymptote. Two further mechanisms push exponents below 3/2 independently of drive. The quasicritical Widom-line picture has input shrink exponents while roughly preserving the scaling relation ([Fosque et al. 2021, *PRL*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02938v1)) [V-abs]. Short-range coupling in a 2D sheet is not mean-field directed percolation ([Zúñiga-Galindo 2026, arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36404)) [V existence; content uncertain, single-author and unreviewed]. Das & Levina also analyse an exactly critical model without homeostasis, whereas the project sits at m < 1.

The discriminating experiment is a drive-scaling family h ∝ N^−β with β ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2}. It should be measured with both causal (per-seed lineage) and binned avalanches, at N reaching at least 10³, alongside a direct estimate of φ^−2 and the overlap fraction 1 − exp(−h·N·⟨T⟩). If binned tails approach 1.25 for β = 1 and 1.5 for β ≥ 1.5, the project confirms Das & Levina in a homeostatic growing network, which is itself publishable. If causal avalanches stay at 1.5 while binned ones drift, the effect is definitional. Note that causal avalanches can be scale-free off-criticality ([Martinello et al. 2017, *PRX*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.7.041071)) [U].

## Fractal dimension is a weak lever, and D = 2.5 cannot exist in a plane

### Measured neuron D sits between about 1.1 and 1.7 in 2D, over barely one decade

Box-counting D of a planar image is bounded by 2, so **D = 2.5 is not a meaningful target for 2D box-counting**. In the generative rule r = b^(−1/D) it is simply a length-ratio parameter (r ≈ 0.76 for b = 2), not a dimension. D = 1.8 and D = 2.5 may also produce similar realized box-counting values near the space-filling limit.

The empirical range is about 1.3–1.5 for cortical and hippocampal pyramidal arbors and about 1.6–1.8 for dense retinal or cultured cells. Rat CA1 basal arbors give D_A ≈ 1.42–1.46, while individual dendrites are almost straight at D_B ≈ 1.03 ([Rowland et al. 2023, *Front Netw Physiol*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnetp.2023.1072815)) [V]. Retinal neurons in culture gave DLA-like values of about 1.7 ([Caserta et al. 1990, *PRL*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.64.95); value as summarized in [Werner 2010, *Front Physiol*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2741)) [V-exists / V].

Scaling holds over only about 4–40 µm, roughly one decade, so D is an "effective" dimension [V]. Panico & Sterling argued that retinal arbors "are not fractal but space-filling", with D → 2 down to a cutoff, and showed that non-fractal growth rules produce equally "fractal-looking" measurements ([Panico & Sterling 1995, *J Comp Neurol*](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.903610311)) [V]. Method sensitivity (resolution, box-size range, projection, skeletonization) is a standing caution ([Jelinek & Fernández 1998, *J Neurosci Methods*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0165-0270(98)00021-1)) [U]. Any claim that the simulation "has D = x" therefore needs box-counting of the generated images over a matched 1–1.5-decade range with multiple grid offsets, not inference from the generative parameter.

### Taylor's "optimal D" concerns single-neuron reach, not network dynamics

Taylor's group argues that neurons sit near a peak of a geometric connectivity-to-cost ratio. They tested about 1,600 distorted CA1 models ([Smith et al. 2021, *Sci Rep*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-81421-2)) [V] and found arbor D raised by enrichment ([Rowland et al. 2022, *Front Physiol*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2022.932598)) [V]. Two points cut against using this for the project. The "connectivity" is single-neuron proximity per unit material, not network dynamics, and no independent replication was found. More practically, D_A is about 4× more sensitive to dendrite tortuosity ("weaving") than one might expect: D_B falling from 1.02 to 1.01 moved D_A from 1.42 to 1.46 ([Rowland et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnetp.2023.1072815)) [V]. A model that sets D only through an inter-branch length ratio leaves out the main contributor to measured D.

### Wiring theory and homeostasis both predict the project's morphology null

Wiring-optimization theory treats arbor *territory* and *target density* as the controls. D is a derived descriptor ([Cuntz et al. 2010, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000877)) [V-exists], ([Cuntz, Mathy & Häusser 2012, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1200430109)) [U]. Contact probability follows overlap of length densities (Peters'-rule logic; [Chklovskii 2004, *Neuron*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2004.08.012)) [U].

More decisively, in every growth-to-criticality model **homeostatic growth tunes total effective coupling until the rate hits target**, and geometry only distributes that coupling ([Tetzlaff et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001013); [Kossio et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02861)) [V]. The researchers' series estimate shows that D = 1.3 versus 2.5 changes per-order cable growth by 1.17× versus 1.52×, roughly doubling total length over five orders. Homeostatic growth and retraction should simply resize arbors until the same in-coupling is reached. That explains why both criticality *and* graph statistics barely move.

An experimental analogue exists. Okujeni & Egert found that long-term Ca²⁺ influx converged across cultures despite "very different architectures, neurite extent, connectivity, modularity and activity" ([Okujeni & Egert 2019, *eLife*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.47996)) [V-abs]. The morphology null is therefore defensible as a result ("homeostatic growth makes network dynamics geometry-invariant") but not as a headline.

### Mature simulators exist; none sweeps D against dynamics

NETMORPH builds stochastic 2D/3D networks with synapses wherever axon–dendrite segments fall within a distance criterion ([Koene et al. 2009, *Neuroinformatics*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12021-009-9052-3)) [V-exists]. DeNSE is a 2D growth platform integrated with NEST and is the closest existing "2D growth → contact connectivity → dynamics" pipeline ([DeNSE, GitHub](https://github.com/SENeC-Initiative/DeNSE)) [V]. CX3D and BioDynaMo ([Zubler & Douglas 2009](https://doi.org/10.3389/neuro.10.025.2009); [Breitwieser et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab649)) [U] and NeuroDevSim ([bioRxiv 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.10.17.512465.full.pdf)) [V-exists] are the other options. Culture models identify **connection range, anisotropy, clustering and modularity**, not dendritic D, as the spatial features that change dynamics ([Okujeni & Egert 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.47996)) [V-abs], ([Houben, Garcia-Ojalvo & Soriano 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04427)) [V]. On the theory side, hierarchical-modular structure stretches criticality into Griffiths phases ([Moretti & Muñoz 2013, *Nat Commun*](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms3521)) [U]. Active dendritic trees enhance single-neuron dynamic range, with larger trees giving larger range ([Gollo, Kinouchi & Copelli 2009, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000402)) [V], but that is criticality *within* an arbor. A search for dendrite D combined with avalanches and morphology simulation returned nothing [V, negative result]. The gap is real, but the literature predicts that D affects criticality only through connection range, degree heterogeneity and Λ.

## Cultures and in vivo recordings supply trajectories, set points and dynamic-range curves

### Developing cultures pass through a burst-dominated phase before near-criticality

No longitudinal culture study finds criticality from the outset. Tetzlaff et al. report initial (about 19 DIV), supercritical (about 20–22 DIV), subcritical (about 36 DIV) and critical (about 58 DIV) states in 20 cultures on 59-electrode MEAs, though only partly longitudinally ([Tetzlaff et al. 2010](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001013)) [V]. Yada et al. found exponential, then bimodal (a single network burst), then power-law-like statistics over 4–30 DIV on HD-CMOS arrays, an "integration then fragmentation" sequence ([Yada et al. 2017, *Neuroscience*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2016.11.031)) [V-abs]. Pasquale et al. observed sub-, super- and critical states depending on age, with criticality coinciding with medium burst synchronization and a high burst-to-spike ratio ([Pasquale et al. 2008, *Neuroscience*](https://www.pubmed.gov/18448256/)) [V-abs].

The most reproducible target is therefore **the shape of the trajectory, especially a transient burst-dominated overshoot**, not exact DIV values. A homeostatic growth model that approaches criticality monotonically from below disagrees with these data. More recent work shows a gradual sub- to near-critical transition over 10–35 DIV ([Zhang et al. 2025, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00764)) [V-abs, preprint]. iPSC networks need not converge at all ([Heiney et al., arXiv:1907.02351](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02351)) [V-abs].

To calibrate model time, synapses per neuron in rat cortical culture rise from **under 5 at 7 DIV to about 150–400 at 21 DIV**, with more synapses per neuron at lower density ([Cullen et al. 2010, *Brain Res*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2969840)) [V-abs]. This bears on the granularity result. With hundreds of synapses per neuron, one contact moves a neuron's input coupling by well under 1%. The project's per-contact quantum at N = 25–200 is likely an order of magnitude coarser (my inference), so the precision-floor law should be stated in contacts per neuron and extrapolated to realistic values.

### In vivo, criticality behaves like a homeostatic set point that leads firing-rate recovery

In freely behaving rat V1, monocular deprivation disrupted criticality, which recovered over **48 h and more than 30 h before firing-rate homeostasis**. Modelling pointed to inhibitory plasticity and architecture rather than excitatory rate homeostasis ([Ma, Turrigiano, Wessel & Hengen 2019, *Neuron*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6934140)) [V-abs]. This is a direct challenge to any model in which criticality is a by-product of a firing-rate set point, which predicts the opposite order. Waking progressively degrades criticality and sleep restores it ([Xu, Schneider, Wessel & Hengen 2024, *Nat Neurosci*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01536-9)) [V]. Hengen & Shew frame criticality as a unified set point and report a meta-analysis of 140 datasets. They claim a long-standing controversy is "the product of a methodological choice" ([Hengen & Shew 2025, *Neuron*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.05.020)) [V-abs; the specific choice is unverified].

Dynamic range peaks near criticality in organotypic cultures with pharmacologically shifted E/I ([Shew et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3864-09.2009)) [V-abs] and in anesthetized barrel cortex. The latter model needed activity-dependent depression to fit the data ([Gautam et al. 2015, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004576)) [V-abs]. These are the tuning curves against which the project's dynamic-range result can be placed. Engineered modular cultures show dynamical richness peaking at intermediate coupling, "at the verge of physical disconnection" ([Yamamoto et al. 2018, *Sci Adv*](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau4914)) [V-abs].

## Referees will demand Λ, nulls and finite-size scaling before "critical"

The field now treats power laws, and even the crackling-noise relation, as **necessary but not sufficient**. Non-critical Brunel E/I networks and stochastic surrogates pass the standard battery ([Destexhe & Touboul 2021, *eNeuro*](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0551-20.2021)) [V]. Slowly varying drive mimics power laws ([Priesemann & Shriki 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006081)) [V]. Multiple latent variables produce avalanche scaling "without fine-tuning" ([Morrell, Nemenman & Sederberg 2024, *eLife*](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.89337)) [V]. Overlapping electrode fields make non-critical networks look critical ([Neto, Spitzner & Priesemann 2022, *PLoS Comput Biol*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9733887)) [V]. Referees may also ask whether the homeostatic variable itself acts as a slow latent field, which is a natural objection to a hovering network.

The accepted vocabulary is "near-critical", "reverberating", "quasicritical" and "distance to criticality" ([Wilting & Priesemann 2019, *Curr Opin Neurobiol*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2019.08.002)) [V], ([O'Byrne & Jerbi 2022, *Trends Neurosci*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tins.2022.08.007)) [V]. Proponents ([Plenz et al. 2021, *Front Phys*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2021.639389)) [V] and critics ([Sipling, Zhang & Di Ventra 2026, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21071)) [V] both remain active.

The project's four pitfalls map onto known fixes. **P1** (KS xmin): use doubly truncated MLE with range chosen by maximizing log(x_max/x_min) subject to a goodness-of-fit p-value, plus exponent-stability plots and likelihood-ratio tests against lognormal and truncated alternatives ([Deluca & Corral 2013, *Acta Geophys*](https://doi.org/10.2478/s11600-013-0154-9); [Clauset, Shalizi & Newman 2009, *SIAM Rev*](https://doi.org/10.1137/070710111)) [U]. Curvature should be reported as distance from criticality, not fitted away. **P2** (epoch mixtures): show avalanche distributions conditioned on instantaneous σ or m, which is the direct answer to Priesemann & Shriki. **P3** (overlap): report causal and binned avalanches and the overlap fraction (Das & Levina 2019 [V]; Martinello 2017 [U]). **P4** (σ ≠ m): the critical quantity is the **largest eigenvalue Λ** of the transmission matrix, not the mean column sum ([Larremore et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.106.058101)) [V-abs]. Measured m departs further from Λ through coalescence and saturation (1 − ∏(1 − p) < Σp), refractoriness, input contamination and bin width ([Zierenberg et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10402)) [V-abs]. The recommended report is σ, Λ, causal m, naive and input-corrected regression m, and multistep-regression m ([Wilting & Priesemann 2018, *Nat Commun*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04725-4)) [U].

The model-only gold standards are what let a simulation paper exceed experiments. First, locate the transition independently: on frozen grown snapshots, sweep a global gain to find g_c via autocorrelation time, susceptibility, dynamic range and single-seed survival, then report where homeostasis places the network (1 − Λ, g/g_c). Second, run finite-size scaling over at least a decade of N, with N-independent exponents, cutoffs scaling as powers of N, and collapse. Third, test the scaling relation γ = (α−1)/(τ−1) with three independently fitted exponents plus shape collapse ([Friedman et al. 2012, *PRL*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.108.208102)) [U], and report the deviation from criticality coefficient (DCC) ([Ma et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2019.08.031)) [V-abs]. Fourth, run at least two matched non-critical nulls through the identical pipeline: inhomogeneous Poisson driven by the model's own rate trajectory, a latent-variable model, and the grown network rescaled to Λ = 0.9 and Λ = 1.1. Finally, subsample the model to 1–50% for comparison with recordings ([Levina & Priesemann 2017, *Nat Commun*](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms15140)) [U].

## Prioritized next steps and candidate paper framings

The steps below are ordered so that each cheap measurement gates the next decision. Steps 1–3 are prerequisites for any paper. Steps 4–5 carry the novelty.

| Priority | Step | What it decides | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compute Λ, causal m, input-corrected and multistep-regression m, and the Zierenberg 2020 corrected estimator on existing runs. Quantify each gap from σ | Whether "σ ≠ m" becomes a result. Which estimator drives the m(N) residual (1 − m ∝ N^−0.7 vs the predicted N^−1) | Low |
| 2 | Frozen-snapshot gain sweep: locate g_c via autocorrelation time, variance, dynamic range and seed survival. Place homeostatic and slow/fast-growth networks on this axis | Whether any regime is critical rather than reverberating. Gives the x-axis for every later plot | Low–medium |
| 3 | Drive-scaling family h ∝ N^−β (β = 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2), N up to ≥ 10³, causal and binned avalanches, overlap fraction, estimated φ^−2 crossover. Also locate runs relative to Zierenberg's bursting line h/r* ≈ Δt·k̄/τ_hp | Resolves the Das & Levina 5/4 conflict. Separates drive effects from hovering effects | Medium |
| 4 | **Granularity experiment**: vary the per-contact coupling quantum Δσ_c at fixed target, crossed with growth rate K / sensor τ_C and N. Measure Var(σ), its spectrum (ω vs Kinouchi's τ^−1/2), the time-averaged offset, and the conditional avalanche ratio. Fit a joint scaling law and extrapolate to 150–400 contacts per neuron | The core novel result: a precision floor set by Δσ_c, K/τ_C and N, positioned against Kinouchi's O(1/τ) and the Bonachela/Kinouchi N-dependence dispute | Medium–high |
| 5 | **Shape–function dissociation**: conditional distributions binned by instantaneous Λ/m. Dynamic range vs fraction of time supercritical. Nulls: inhomogeneous Poisson with the matched rate trajectory, a frozen network at mean σ, and a latent-variable model | Shows whether fast-growth 3/2 laws are mixture artefacts while function tracks steady proximity | Medium |
| 6 | Refit everything with doubly truncated MLE, stability plots, LR tests, γ scaling relation, shape collapse and DCC, with ≥ 10 seeds and bootstrap CIs | Referee-proofing | Low |
| 7 | Morphology triage: box-count realized D, drop D = 2.5, restrict to 1.3–1.7, rename the parameter, hold total length and span fixed, add tortuosity. Measure p(d), degree variance and Λ | Whether morphology stays a main axis (likely not) or becomes a robustness section | Low–medium |
| 8 | Developmental trajectory: check for a connectivity or bursting overshoot, calibrate time by synapses per neuron, compare with Wagenaar data subsampled to 59 electrodes | Adds empirical anchoring. A monotone approach would conflict with culture data | Medium |
| 9 | Optional: add inhibition and depression, and test Ma 2019's ordering (criticality recovers before rate) | Extends to in vivo set-point claims | High |

The strongest framing combines steps 4 and 5: **"How precisely can homeostatic growth tune a network to criticality, and do power laws measure it?"** The claim would be that structural homeostasis has an irreducible precision floor set by contact granularity, growth rate relative to the activity sensor, and N. Within that floor, the regime that produces the textbook 3/2 law (fast growth, large excursions, pooled epochs) is not the regime that maximizes dynamic range (slow growth, pinned near the reverberating fixed point). This framing is novel against everything found. It uses SOqC theory (Kinouchi 2019; Bonachela 2009/2010), the homeostasis–input theory (Zierenberg 2018), Kossio's growing networks and the critique literature (Priesemann & Shriki 2018; Destexhe & Touboul 2021). It turns the project's artefacts into its results, and it makes a testable prediction for real cultures through synapse counts. Its main risk is a granularity analysis already present in MSP or Butz/van Ooyen avalanche work. A targeted search on "Butz van Ooyen avalanches", "Gallinaro Rotter criticality" and Kossio 2018's citation list should precede writing.

A second, safer framing is **"Finite-size and drive scaling of growth-driven criticality"** (step 3 plus step 1). It is a careful FSS study showing how the time-binned operating point and exponents depend on the drive-scaling exponent β, settling the Das & Levina question for homeostatic networks. It is less novel, because referees will see m = 1 − h/r* as a corollary, but it is publishable if the 5/4 crossover or the m(N) residual is cleanly explained.

A third framing, **"Homeostatic growth makes network dynamics geometry-invariant"**, recasts the morphology null with proper controls (realized D, fixed length and span, tortuosity), the in vitro parallel of Okujeni & Egert's Ca²⁺ convergence, and a demonstration of which spatial features (connection range, heterogeneity via Λ) do break invariance. It fits best as a section of the first paper rather than a standalone, because "fractal dimension does not matter" is expected.

## Conclusion

The project's instinct to grow realistic fractal arbors put its effort into the one axis the physics says homeostasis will erase. Its incidental findings, the hovering, the granularity-set fluctuations, the slow/fast dissociation and the stubborn σ ≠ m gap, point instead to the axis the literature has left empty: the precision and observability of self-tuned criticality. That reframing is what moves the work from "we reproduced Kossio with prettier neurons" to a contribution that the current debate needs, a mechanistic model in which the power law and the function attributed to criticality can be measured separately and shown to diverge. There is a path to something significant. It runs through Λ, causal avalanches and a granularity scaling law, not through D. One afternoon of cheap measurements (steps 1–2) will show whether the m(N) residual and the dynamic-range dissociation survive proper estimators, and that is the go/no-go point.
